# ISO 24495-1 research basis

This note records the public evidence behind the skill. It does not reproduce or
replace ISO 24495-1. The full standard was unavailable, so do not quote it, attribute a
paraphrase to a provision, or cite clauses beyond the public structure below.

## Scope and status

ISO 24495-1:2023, _Plain language - Part 1: Governing principles and guidelines_,
is a 14-page guidance standard published in June 2023. ISO/TC 37, Language and
terminology, prepared this first edition.

It covers printed and digital documents made mainly of text. It applies to anyone who
creates documents, including public, technical, legislative, and controlled-language
work. It is designed for most written languages, but its examples are in English.

The public ISO preview identifies this structure:

- clauses 1 to 3: scope, normative references, and terms and definitions
- clause 4: four governing principles
- clause 5: guideline groups for relevant, findable, understandable, and usable content
- Annex A: overview of the principles and guidelines
- Annex B: sample checklist

The standard has no normative references. It does not provide complete technical
guidance for accessibility or digital documents, although its principles can support
that work.

## Core model

The standard adopts the International Plain Language Federation's reader-centered
definition: wording, structure, and design work together so intended readers can find,
understand, and use the information they need.

The four principles are:

1. Readers get what they need: relevant.
2. Readers can easily find what they need: findable.
3. Readers can easily understand what they find: understandable.
4. Readers can easily use the information: usable.

Readers may read, scan, listen to, or otherwise use a document. A document can serve
several audiences; the public preview gives the primary audience priority when needs
conflict.

The model measures reader outcomes, not mechanical features such as readability scores.
The Federation reports that only about 7% of the standard's roughly 485 ideas concern
word choice. The skill therefore starts with reader needs and document structure.

## Evaluation implications

The ISO preview defines evaluation by how well readers find, understand, and use
information. Editorial review can identify likely barriers; intended readers provide
stronger evidence.

Supporting public-sector guidance recommends:

- testing as soon as content is available
- realistic usability tasks for finding and using information
- paraphrase or teach-back interviews
- revision and retesting
- smaller tests before comparative studies when warranted

Focus groups and preference questions reveal attitudes, not whether readers can find,
understand, or use information.

## Important distinctions

- **Plain language is audience-specific.** A familiar technical term can be plainer for
  experts than an everyday paraphrase.
- **Plain language preserves meaning.** Necessary detail, precision, conditions, and
  legal effect remain.
- **Plain language is not easy language.** The ISO introduction distinguishes plain
  language from methods for people with reading-comprehension difficulties.
- **Plain language is not accessibility conformance.** Pair it with applicable
  accessibility standards, such as WCAG for web content.
- **Part 1 does not certify documents.** It is guidance, with no route for certifying an
  individual document. Editorial review, expert approval, or reader testing does not
  make a document "ISO 24495-1 certified."
- **Other parts are separate.** Part 2 covers legal communication, and Part 3 covers
  science writing. As of the research date, ISO was developing Part 4 to support
  certification that an organization uses plain language systematically. It would not
  certify individual documents under Part 1. This is not an exhaustive list.

## Source boundaries

1. **ISO:** title, status, scope, definitions, principles, and public document structure.
2. **International Plain Language Federation:** history, development, implementation
   context, research bibliography, and explanations of the principles.
3. **Supporting guidance:** concrete drafting, design, and testing techniques from
   Digital.gov and similar public sources.

Attribute only material visible on public ISO pages, including the introduction, scope,
definitions, four principles, and document structure, directly to ISO. Present
individual drafting, design, and testing techniques as supporting practice, not ISO
provisions.

Reject secondary summaries that rename the principles. `Consistent` and `actionable`
can describe useful techniques, but they do not replace `relevant` and `usable`.
