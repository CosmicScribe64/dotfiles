# Claims, tone, and formatting checks

Use when the issue is a claim's substance or presentation, not just word choice.

## Inflated significance and sales language

Claims of historic importance, universal benefit, or exceptional beauty need a basis
and a purpose in the genre. Separate attributed opinion from established results;
advertising may be enthusiastic without inventing facts. "Statistically significant"
is not interchangeable with promotional "significant."

## Name-dropping and borrowed authority

Ask how publications, awards, follower counts, or historical analogies support the point.
An explicit summary may omit peripheral examples under the core's selection rules;
ordinary tightening does not silently discard their facts. Keep the distinction between
one source, several sources, and verified consensus. "Experts argue" needs a source,
not a fabricated name or a stronger unattributed claim.

## Knowledge limits and speculative gap-filling

Remove irrelevant model-centered disclaimers, not meaningful "as of" dates or research
limitations. Missing biographical information does not establish a motive or a desire
for privacy. "The supplied biography omits her early life" is narrower than "her early
life is not publicly documented."

Keep unknowns unknown. Flag speculation separately while improving surrounding wording;
do not stall an entire rewrite because a claim lacks detail. A more specific implication,
number, study, or causal mechanism requires evidence.

## Manufactured insights and concept labels

"The deeper issue," "X is the currency of Y," and labels such as "the acceleration
trap" may announce analysis without supplying it. Describe the supported relationship.
Keep a useful defined concept, but do not imply an invented label is established or
invent an explanation to replace a vague metaphor.

## Formatting in context

| Feature                      | Decision                                                                                                               |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Em dashes                    | Remove from prose; use a period or comma, not parentheses or en dashes. Keep ranges, minus signs, and command flags.   |
| Colons                       | Keep before lists, examples, quotations, and labels; rewrite colons that join two clauses.                             |
| Bold text or labeled bullets | Remove competing emphasis and restating labels; keep labels that help readers find fields or warnings.                 |
| Heading capitalization       | Sentence case unless a documented house style says otherwise; preserve proper nouns, acronyms, and stable anchors.     |
| Emojis or arrows             | Remove from headings, bullets, and prose; keep meaningful notation and directional information in diagrams or tables.  |
| Curly quotes                 | Straighten, except where the language or format requires other marks or the text is protected.                        |
| Hyphenated compounds         | Follow grammar and established terms, not a rule that every pair is hyphenated or every predicative hyphen disappears. |
